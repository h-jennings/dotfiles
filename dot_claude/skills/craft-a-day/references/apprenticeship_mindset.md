# The Apprenticeship Mindset

Craft A Day is built on the spirit of *Apprenticeship Patterns* (Hoover &
Oshineye): a craftsperson stays sharp through deliberate, humble, repeated
practice — not credentials, not cramming. These are the patterns that drive this
skill. Keep them in the back of your mind when selecting exercises, framing
sessions, and giving feedback. Apply the *ideas*; don't lecture the user about
them.

## Breakable Toys

Build small, throwaway versions of real things in a safe space where failure is
free. A from-scratch debounce or LRU cache is a breakable toy — the point isn't
the artifact, it's what your hands learn making it. → The `build` track is this
pattern. Encourage building the real primitive, not importing it.

## Practice, Practice, Practice

Deliberate practice means repeated reps in a low-stakes setting, with room to be
slow and wrong. Getting stuck or fumbling is the practice working, not failing. →
The whole skill is this. Normalize struggle; never rush a rep.

## Confront Your Ignorance

Pick something you don't know well and deliberately close the gap. The skill's
spaced-repetition nudges and the user's stated weak spots are the raw material. →
When choosing exercises, lean slightly toward the rusty edge, not just the
comfortable groove.

## Expose Your Ignorance

Say "I don't know this yet" out loud — it's how learning starts. → Make the
session a place where not-knowing is welcome. Invite the user to name what feels
shaky; turn that into the next exercise.

## Reflect As You Work

Pause to ask "what just happened, and what would I do differently?" Insight comes
from examined practice, not raw volume. → Every Practice session ends with a
one-line reflection (the `## Reflection` block + `learnings.md`). Don't skip it.

## Record What You Learn

Keep a running log of insights so they compound instead of evaporating. →
`learnings.md` is the logbook. Append one honest line per session; over months it
becomes a map of how the craft grew.

## Use the Source

Read other people's code to absorb how good code is built. → Reach for this in
the `refactor` and `debugging` tracks (read closely before you change anything),
and when adding the worked reference solution — treat it as a thing to study, not
just an answer key.

## The White Belt

Approach even familiar material as a beginner, setting aside "I already know
this." Mastery hides in the basics. → The `fluency` track is white-belt work:
drilling the fundamentals you think you've outgrown is exactly where fluency is
kept.

## Nurture Your Passion

Protect the part of you that loves the craft from the grind of day-to-day product
work. A short, enjoyable morning rep is a deposit in that account. → Keep
sessions light and rewarding. Celebrate clean code as much as a correct answer.
This is the skill's core purpose: keep the craft *alive*, not just maintained.
